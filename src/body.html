Worn in memory of the land before us

The collectionVagari Vati, by Jessian Titus

This collection is Jessian Titus’s tribute to his ancestors and to their land in Africa.

It is cut loose, for a city a long way from that land. What carries the memory across is the print.

Eleven prints. Each one says something.

The patternsSorted into four families

In much of West Africa, cloth is a place to keep memory. On Malian mud cloth, bògòlanfini, each mark is painted by hand in fermented mud and stands for something its maker knows: a place, a saying, a passage in a life.

Most of the prints here are made of the same kinds of shapes. A few, the florals and the paisley, came by other roads, and we say so where they do.

Choose a print. Its story opens.

A record you can wear

Ancestry

A pattern like these is repeated by hand, row after row, by someone who learned it from someone else. Wearing it keeps that chain going one more link.

Each piece is named for an idea worth carrying, and printed with the marks of the people who carried it first.

Grid

24 columns, no gutters, one margin. Text keeps to 6 columns at an edge. An open photo takes 8. What is left stays empty.

The film and the product page each fill one screen. Only the wall of pieces scrolls.

Wall

Pieces hang on black, cut out of their background, five across, with wide margins and space between them. Behind them runs the Maitri print, faded, grainy and slipping.

A small cross marks every corner and the place you are. No overlays, no frames, no logo on the photographs.
